Lesion removal

Mole & Spot Removal Laser

점·병변 제거 레이저

A laser that gently shaves off lesions like moles, warts, and age spots.

Is it for you?

  • You want moles or age spots removed
  • You have flat warts or milia
  • Raised spots bother you
  • You want them gone in one or two sessions

What it is

Instead of breaking up pigment, these lasers shave off the lesion itself in thin layers. CO2 (10,600nm) and Er:YAG (2,940nm) lasers are absorbed by water in the skin and vaporize tissue.

Er:YAG is absorbed by water even more strongly, so there's less heat damage around it and recovery tends to be faster. These lasers are mainly used on raised lesions like moles, age spots, flat warts, and milia.

What it may help with

  • Removing moles and age spots
  • Removing flat warts, milia, and other lesions
  • Flattening raised lesions
  • Er:YAG may also help smooth skin texture

At a glance

Treatment time
About 5–15 min (depending on the number of spots)
Numbing
Usually local anesthesia (cream or injection)
Downtime
Scabs form and usually fall off over 1–2 weeks
How often
Sometimes one session; deeper or larger lesions may need several (varies by person)
How long it lasts
Removed lesions rarely come back, but warts and similar lesions can recur

Why prices vary

Mole and lesion removal is usually priced by number and size. Even for the same "mole removal," pricing differs a lot between per-spot and flat-rate (unlimited) plans. The device (CO2 or Er:YAG) and lesion depth also matter.

What to expect

  1. 1The type and depth of each lesion are assessed.
  2. 2Local anesthesia is applied.
  3. 3The lesion is gently shaved off with the laser.
  4. 4Ointment is applied, and you're shown how to care for the healing tape and scab.

Side effects & precautions

  • Scabs will form. Picking them can leave scars or dark spots, so let them fall off on their own.
  • After the scab falls off, red marks or dark spots may linger for a while and fade over time.
  • Deep lesions may not come off in one go, or may be removed in stages to minimize scarring.
  • Viral lesions like warts can come back.
  • Skipping sun protection and aftercare during recovery raises the risk of dark spots.

FAQ

Q Will mole removal leave a scar?
Shallow removals often leave a faint mark that fades, but deeper ones carry a risk of scarring. Not picking the scab is key.
Q Can this remove tattoos?
Ablative lasers shave the skin, so they're rarely used for tattoos. Pigment lasers (pico or Q-switched) are a better fit.

This is general information about the treatment. Results and side effects vary from person to person. Always decide on treatment in consultation with a medical professional.
